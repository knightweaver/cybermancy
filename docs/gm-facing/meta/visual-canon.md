# Visual Canon

# CYBERMANCY VISUAL CANON

**Status:** Working Canon
**Scope:** Cybermancy visual production for Foundry VTT assets, character/class/subclass art, adversaries, environments, domains, and supporting generated imagery.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual language of **Cybermancy** so artwork created at different times, by different prompts, or through different generation pipelines still feels like it belongs to one coherent world.

The governing principle is **grounded cyberpunk rather than spectacle-first cyberpunk**. Cybermancy should look like a plausible, lived-in dystopian future in which extraordinary technology, cybernetics, violence, magic, and social fragmentation have become part of ordinary material culture.

The visual canon should preserve:

* human presence and psychological realism;
* believable technology and materials;
* restrained but distinctive cyberpunk lighting;
* clear social and economic stratification;
* selective visual strangeness rather than constant visual noise;
* strong readability appropriate to the intended asset type.

Different asset families have different requirements. A feature icon, a subclass portrait, an adversary token, and a cinematic environment image should **not** all use the same prompt structure.

---

# 2. Global Cybermancy Visual Language

## 2.1 Core Aesthetic

Use this as the default visual-language block for Cybermancy character portraits and scenes:

> Photorealistic or semi-photorealistic grounded cyberpunk with subtle dystopian futurism. Human-centered composition with psychological presence and lived-in detail. Moody cinematic atmosphere, believable materials, restrained but striking technology, and environments that feel inhabited rather than staged.
>
> Lighting uses cool teal-blue ambient tones contrasted with selective warm amber-orange practical light, neon spill, reflective surfaces, and restrained bloom. Shadows are rich but readable. Maintain balanced luminance and avoid oversaturation.
>
> Wardrobe blends contemporary utilitarian fashion, corporate tailoring, streetwear, technical fabrics, worn leather, matte polymers, discreet armor, and subtle cybernetic integration. Technology should feel functional, expensive or improvised according to context, and slightly worn rather than glossy or toy-like.
>
> Characters should appear psychologically real. Faces may show fatigue, discipline, suspicion, grief, intelligence, resilience, quiet menace, or competence under pressure. Beauty is optional; character is mandatory.
>
> Color palettes are generally built from charcoal, grey, black, steel blue, weathered concrete, and muted natural materials with controlled accent colors from implants, signage, holograms, circuitry, weapons, and practical lighting.
>
> Camera language is cinematic: realistic lensing, clear focal subject, atmospheric depth, selective shallow depth of field, haze where appropriate, and a strong distinction between foreground subject and lived-in background.
>
> Tone: elegant decay, high technology, social inequality, competence under pressure, and humanity surviving inside machinery.

## 2.2 Technology

Cybermancy technology should look **integrated into life**, not pasted onto it.

Good motifs include:

* temple ports and cranial interfaces;
* ocular modifications;
* prosthetic limbs;
* neural seams;
* subdermal circuitry or light traces;
* wearable interface hardware;
* tactical communications equipment;
* worn industrial robotics;
* functional cybernetic replacements;
* unobtrusive corporate-grade implants;
* jury-rigged street technology.

Avoid adding glowing cybernetic seams simply because a character is cyberpunk. Every visible technological element should imply a function.

## 2.3 Materials

Favored materials include:

* brushed and blackened metal;
* carbon fiber;
* reinforced polymer;
* technical textile;
* worn leather;
* glass;
* ceramic armor;
* composite panels;
* painted industrial steel;
* concrete;
* wet asphalt;
* aged plastic;
* restrained illuminated circuitry.

Surface wear matters. Even premium technology should generally feel touched, used, maintained, repaired, or lived with.

## 2.4 Lighting

The default Cybermancy lighting grammar is:

* cool teal/cyan/steel-blue ambient light;
* one restrained warm counterpoint such as amber, orange, or sodium-vapor light;
* practical sources should usually motivate visible color;
* faces and silhouettes remain readable;
* avoid crushing every scene into darkness;
* avoid making every object glow.

The setting benefits from darkness, but **muddy luminance is not visual sophistication**. Important forms should remain legible.

## 2.5 Negative Guidance

Avoid:

* cartoon or anime treatment unless explicitly requested;
* superhero-costume aesthetics;
* generic space marine armor;
* glossy plastic;
* excessive chrome;
* unexplained neon strips on every object;
* rainbow cyberpunk palettes;
* overly sterile utopian futurism;
* random HUD overlays;
* meaningless pseudo-text;
* exaggerated action poses where a quieter composition would communicate the character better;
* generic “sci-fi soldier” imagery when the subject has a more specific social role.

# 3. Asset Families

## 3.1 Item and Feature Icons

Item and feature icons use the original Cybermancy icon language.

### Standard

* **Working/output size:** 400 × 400 px
* **Preferred format:** `.webp`
* **Background:** light grey
* **Readability target:** clear at approximately 64–128 px
* **No text or labels**
* **Clean silhouette**
* **Semi-realistic rendering**
* **Metallic, glass, polymer, textile, or other physically plausible materials**
* **Soft reflections**
* **Subtle vignette**
* **Consistent luminance across the set**

A successful icon should read first as a single object or single action-symbol and only secondarily reveal small details.

### Prompt Style

> Cyberpunk item or feature icon, semi-realistic rendering, physically believable materials, clean silhouette, soft reflections, subtle vignette, no text or labels, light grey background, balanced luminance, readable at small icon scale.

### Composition

Prefer:

* one dominant object;
* one visually obvious action;
* limited secondary elements;
* restrained glow;
* simple background;
* visual mass near the center.

Avoid environmental scenes masquerading as icons.

# 4. Class and Subclass Art

Class and subclass imagery is **not an enlarged item icon**.

Earlier approaches that reused the item-icon style led to generic armored figures and over-simplified emblem compositions. Class/subclass art should instead communicate a **human archetype**.

## 4.1 Standard

Class and subclass artwork may be substantially larger than feature icons and may contain richer detail.

Use:

* medium portrait, bust, three-quarter, or full-body character composition;
* visible face when appropriate;
* expressive posture;
* signature equipment;
* wardrobe that conveys social function;
* cinematic but restrained lighting;
* enough environmental context to suggest role, but not enough to compete with the figure.

### Prompt Style

> Semi-photorealistic Cybermancy character archetype portrait. Grounded cyberpunk, human-centered composition, psychologically believable face and posture, functional futuristic clothing and equipment, physically plausible cybernetics, cinematic cool ambient light with restrained warm accent lighting, muted dystopian palette, subtle environmental context, realistic materials, no text, no UI overlay. The image should communicate the identity and social role of the class or subclass rather than resemble a product icon or generic armored soldier.

## 4.2 Character Identity Over Costume

The image should answer:

* Who is this person?
* What do they notice that other people miss?
* What does their role make them wear or carry?
* What kind of danger are they accustomed to?
* What does their profession do to their posture and expression?

Class images should be broad enough to represent multiple character interpretations.

Subclass images may be more specific and archetypal.

# 5. Street Samurai Visual Canon

The Street Samurai is not automatically a plated futuristic warrior.

The class encompasses professional violence, reflex, weapon mastery, situational awareness, and a body modified or trained to survive dangerous environments.

### Palette

**class-streetsamurai**

> Gunmetal and black carbon-fiber base with crimson and steel-blue accents, with faint amber weapon glow where appropriate.

This is a **palette reference**, not a requirement that every Street Samurai wear armor.

---

# 6. Bodyguard Subclass Visual Canon

The Bodyguard is the strongest worked example of why subclass art must be driven by role rather than generic cyberpunk armor.

## 6.1 Archetype

The Bodyguard is a master of protecting a designated person. The archetype may range from an imposing, omnipresent guard to a nearly invisible professional shadow, but the visual narrative is the same:

**the protected person survives.**

## 6.2 Mandatory Visual Cues

Preferred Bodyguard character art should include:

* visible human face;
* **no full combat helmet** in default subclass imagery;
* well-tailored professional clothing rather than heavy battle armor;
* discreet but obvious communications earpiece or radio earbud;
* alert eyes and posture;
* controlled physicality;
* clothing and equipment suitable for executive or close protection;
* subtle defensive or tactical technology rather than conspicuous military hardware.

Useful motifs include:

* scanning a room;
* hand near jacket opening or concealed weapon;
* positioning between danger and another person;
* attention divided between client and surroundings;
* protective stance;
* radio communication;
* calm under active threat.

### Palette

**subclass-bodyguard**

> Matte black and brushed silver with restrained crimson-gold defensive highlights; strong, grounded presence.

Again, this describes the visual family, not mandatory armor.

## 6.3 What to Avoid

Do not default to:

* helmeted riot trooper;
* powered armor;
* faceless black knight;
* generic superhero body armor;
* military assault gear unless the specific image concept demands it.

Those images communicate “soldier” or “riot police,” not “professional protector.”

# 8. Domain Icons

Domain art is a distinct visual family.

A domain icon should be a **symbol**, not a miniature painting and not a rendered object.

## 8.1 Domain SVG Standard

Preferred domain icon characteristics:

* SVG/vector;
* strong silhouette;
* simple geometry;
* black-and-white capable;
* legible at small size;
* centered composition;
* no text;
* no shading required;
* no texture;
* minimal or no perspective;
* negative space used intentionally;
* one primary visual concept.

### Prompt Template

> Create a clean black-and-white SVG icon for the **[DOMAIN]** domain from Cybermancy. The icon should be minimalist, symbolic, geometric, and immediately readable at small size. Use a strong silhouette, solid fills, simple negative space, and no text, labels, gradients, shading, texture, or detailed scene elements. Represent the concept of **[CORE DOMAIN IDEA]** rather than drawing a realistic object. Center the composition in a square viewBox and output valid SVG.

## 8.2 Bullet Domain

The Bullet domain represents firearms, projectile violence, speed, precision, impact, and controlled ballistic force.

Preferred visual concepts:

* projectile in flight;
* ballistic trajectory;
* muzzle-to-impact line;
* stylized projectile + velocity;
* impact geometry;
* a bullet abstracted into a directional symbol.

Avoid a detailed ammunition product render.

### Palette for colored derivative art

**domain-bullet**

> Gunmetal and graphite with fiery orange-to-ember-red glow and restrained cyan tracer accents.

The canonical domain mark should still work in pure black and white.

## 8.3 Maker Domain

The Maker domain represents engineering, fabrication, repair, invention, adaptation, tools, and technological craftsmanship.

Preferred concepts should combine **creation and mechanism** without becoming clip-art.

Possible motif families:

* tool + constructed geometry;
* gear + emerging form;
* fabricated joint;
* modular components becoming whole;
* hand/tool abstraction;
* mechanical assembly symbol.

### Palette for colored derivative art

**domain-maker**

> Titanium grey and steel blue with amber-gold forge glow and teal circuitry accents.

Again, the primary symbol should survive conversion to black and white.

# 10. Adversary Portraits

Adversary portraits belong to a different production family from item icons.

## Standard

* **Default aspect ratio:** 4:5
* cinematic or semi-photorealistic;
* believable biological, human, or technological subject first;
* Cybermancy corruption, cybernetics, Resonance effects, mutations, or ritual elements second;
* strong silhouette;
* atmospheric environment;
* readable identity;
* no text;
* no labels;
* no stat blocks;
* no UI;
* no mock package layout.

The underlying organism/person/machine should remain understandable before supernatural or technological corruption is layered onto it.

---

# 11. Adversary Tokens

Tokens must represent the **same identity** as the portrait rather than a generic replacement image.

## Standard

* **Aspect ratio:** 1:1
* reference production size: approximately **1254 × 1254 px**
* tight face/body composition readable at token scale;
* thick distressed dark-metal circular rim nearly touching the edges;
* exterior corners outside the circular token remain solid black;
* no text;
* no labels;
* no logos;
* no transparent corner treatment.

Approved visual precedents include:

* **Sewerjaw Gator**
* **Liminal Research Drone**

The token must remain recognizable at Foundry table scale.

---

# 12. Environment Art

Environment artwork is cinematic establishing art, not a diagram.

## Standard

* **Default aspect ratio:** 3:2 landscape
* cinematic or semi-photorealistic;
* strong establishing composition;
* useful for encounter or exploration context;
* no text;
* no stat block;
* no UI overlays.

Before generating an important environment, specify:

* architecture or geology;
* decay state;
* ecology;
* primary lighting source;
* required details;
* forbidden details;
* relationship to nearby locations.

This prevents environmental drift and generic “cyberpunk alley #847.”

---

# 13. Scene and NPC Portrait Guidance

Successful Cybermancy NPC and scene imagery tends to share the following qualities:

### People

* distinctive age, ancestry, profession, and life history;
* real skin texture;
* believable clothing;
* limited but meaningful augmentation;
* composure more often than theatrical posing;
* facial expression tied to narrative role;
* asymmetry and lived experience where appropriate.

### Environments

Common successful environmental cues include:

* megacity density;
* rain;
* wet pavement;
* industrial interiors;
* arcologies;
* repair shops;
* diners and cafés;
* dim workshops;
* corporate interiors;
* underground infrastructure;
* aging public systems;
* atmospheric signage rather than walls of unreadable neon.

# 14. Faction and Social-Class Modifiers

## Corporate

Use:

* controlled posture;
* precise tailoring;
* clean premium materials;
* subtle branding;
* restrained white/black/gold or corporate-specific palette;
* sterile or luxurious architecture;
* technology that is integrated and expensive rather than improvised.

Power should often be communicated through **restraint**.

## Street / Barrens

Use:

* layered clothing;
* repair seams;
* improvised equipment;
* exposed utility hardware;
* older technology kept alive;
* graffiti where contextually appropriate;
* mixed-generation materials;
* practical weapons and survival adaptations.

Do not make poverty synonymous with filth or caricature.

## Mystical / Resonance / Uncanny

When the strange enters Cybermancy, keep it selective.

Useful elements include:

* impossible symmetry;
* faint geometric structures;
* luminous eyes;
* data-ghost artifacts;
* subtle spatial distortion;
* probability-like patterns;
* ritual geometry integrated with electronics.

The uncanny works better when ninety percent of the scene remains believable.

# 15. Prompt Construction

A strong Cybermancy prompt should usually contain four layers.

## Layer 1 — Subject

State exactly what is being depicted.

Example:

> A veteran close-protection specialist standing at the edge of a crowded corporate reception, watching the room rather than the camera.

## Layer 2 — Narrative Function

State what the viewer should understand.

> She is protecting someone outside the frame and is quietly tracking multiple possible threats.

## Layer 3 — Cybermancy Visual Canon

Apply the global visual-language block or the appropriate abbreviated version.

## Layer 4 — Asset-Specific Constraints

Examples:

### Item icon

> 400×400 icon composition, clean silhouette, light grey background, no text.

### Subclass art

> Human-centered character archetype portrait, visible face, richer detail than an icon.

### Token

> Circular dark-metal token rim, tight composition, readable at Foundry scale.

### Environment

> 3:2 cinematic establishing image with specified architecture and lighting.

---

# 16. Short Reusable Canon Prompt

For situations where the full visual block is unnecessarily large:

> Grounded semi-photorealistic Cybermancy aesthetic: believable near-future cyberpunk, psychologically real characters, functional cybernetics, lived-in materials, muted charcoal and steel palette, cool teal-blue ambient light contrasted by restrained amber-orange practical light, cinematic atmospheric depth, selective technological glow, elegant urban decay, social stratification, and high-tech low-life realism. Avoid superhero styling, generic sci-fi armor, glossy plastic, excessive chrome, oversaturated neon, random HUD graphics, and meaningless glowing clutter.

# 17. Relationship to the “Cybermancy Layout”

**Cybermancy visual canon** and the **Cybermancy layout** are related but separate standards.

The **visual canon** governs:

* characters;
* NPC portraits;
* adversaries;
* environments;
* class/subclass art;
* icons;
* tokens;
* domains;
* generated scene imagery.

The **Cybermancy layout** governs illustrated PDFs and editorial presentation.

The established Cybermancy layout uses a polished cinematic cyberpunk editorial / graphic-novel treatment with:

* bold condensed display typography;
* teal/indigo accents;
* atmospheric chapter or section openers;
* generous white-space body pages;
* large integrated illustrations;
* a cohesive high-end ebook / magazine character.

For printable rule material, white or low-ink backgrounds are preferred.

Do **not** infer Foundry asset dimensions, token styling, filenames, data schemas, or asset-generation rules from the PDF layout.

---

# 18. Canon Hierarchy

When visual references disagree, use the following hierarchy:

1. **Explicit later user direction**
2. **Successful approved Cybermancy reference images**
3. **This visual-canon document**
4. **Asset-family standards**
5. **Older generated examples**
6. Generic cyberpunk conventions

A prompt that technically obeys a palette but produces the wrong archetype is not successful canon.

**Role, world logic, and recognizability outrank decorative cyberpunk styling.**

---

# 19. Practical Quality Test

Before accepting new art, ask:

1. Would this still communicate the subject if the neon were removed?
2. Does the technology appear to have a function?
3. Does the person look like they inhabit a society rather than a costume catalog?
4. Does the image communicate the correct profession or narrative role?
5. Is the palette controlled?
6. Are important forms readable?
7. Is there unnecessary armor?
8. Is there unnecessary glow?
9. Does it look like Cybermancy rather than generic cyberpunk?
10. At the intended display size, is the important visual information still obvious?

If several answers are “no,” the image is probably stylish nonsense wearing Cybermancy's coat.

---

# 20. Current Canon Summary

The visual identity of Cybermancy can be reduced to five principles:

**Grounded. Human. Functional. Restrained. Strange at the edges.**

The world contains extraordinary technology and impossible phenomena, but its people still have jobs, bad knees, expensive coats, repaired machinery, social status, obligations, trauma, habits, and reasons for carrying what they carry.

That tension is the visual heart of Cybermancy.
